# Pickleball Tracking Research Using YOLOv8

A research-oriented computer vision project exploring the detection and tracking of pickleball and players in video footage using YOLOv8.

## Overview

Pickleball is a fast-paced sport in which tracking the ball and players can be challenging because of rapid movement, small object size, occlusion, and changing backgrounds.

This project explored how computer vision and object detection could support automated pickleball analysis. The work focused on dataset preparation, object-detection research, model-training experimentation, and investigating the possibilities and limitations of an automated tracking and scoring system.

**Project status:** Research and experimentation — not a fully deployed end-to-end scoring system.

## Objectives

- Explore computer vision techniques for pickleball video analysis.
- Prepare and organize an image dataset with object labels.
- Investigate Roboflow for dataset preparation and annotation workflows.
- Explore YOLOv8 for detecting relevant objects in sports footage.
- Study the challenges of tracking a small, fast-moving ball and identifying players.
- Evaluate the potential for future automated gameplay analysis.

## Technologies and Tools

- **Python** — programming and experimentation
- **YOLOv8** — object-detection model exploration
- **Roboflow** — dataset preparation and annotation workflow
- **OpenCV** — computer-vision and video-processing research
- **Google Colab / Jupyter Notebook** — experimentation environment

These tools are included because they were part of the project's research workflow; the extent of implementation for each component is documented separately.

## Research Workflow

1. **Problem analysis:** Studied the challenges of detecting a pickleball and players in sports footage.
2. **Dataset preparation:** Worked with images and corresponding object labels.
3. **Annotation workflow:** Explored dataset preparation using Roboflow.
4. **Model exploration:** Investigated YOLOv8 as a potential object-detection approach.
5. **Experiment review:** Examined available screenshots and graphs to understand the experimental workflow and its limitations.
6. **System evaluation:** Considered the additional work required for reliable tracking and automated scoring.

## Challenges and Limitations

- Detecting a small ball during rapid movement.
- Handling occlusion and changes in lighting or background.
- Distinguishing relevant players from spectators and other objects.
- Maintaining consistent tracking across video frames.
- Developing reliable gameplay-event detection and scoring logic.
- Moving from individual detection experiments to a complete, real-time system.

The project remained research-oriented. A fully integrated and deployed tracking and scoring application was not completed.

## Future Scope

- Improve detection under different lighting and camera conditions.
- Expand and diversify the dataset.
- Evaluate tracking approaches for consecutive video frames.
- Explore automated detection of serves, rallies, faults, and points.
- Investigate real-time scoreboard generation.
- Study player-performance analytics and processing-speed improvements.
